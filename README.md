
# I. INTRO
## 1. Robotics

### Definition

Robotics is the branch of engineering and computer science that deals with **robots**—machines capable of sensing, thinking, and acting in the physical world.
##### Main Components of a Robot

1. **Actuators** – motors, servos, hydraulic or pneumatic systems
2. **Sensors** – cameras, proximity sensors, force sensors, IMU, EMG, etc.
3. **Controller** – microcontroller, PLC, or computer (Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi)
4. **Software** – algorithms, AI, control logic

### Types of Robots

- **Industrial robots** – welding, painting, assembly (robot arms)
- **Mobile robots** – drones, autonomous cars, delivery robots
- **Humanoid robots** – human-like structure
- **Medical robots** – surgical robots, prosthetics
- **Service robots** – cleaning robots, assistants


## 2. Automation

#### Definition
Automation refers to the use of **control systems and software** to operate machines and processes **with minimal human intervention**.

#### Levels of Automation

1. **Manual** – human does everything
2. **Semi-automatic** – human + machine
3. **Fully automatic** – system runs independently

### Types of Automation

- **Fixed automation** – repetitive tasks (assembly lines)
- **Programmable automation** – batch production (CNC machines)
- **Flexible automation** – adaptable systems (smart factories)

#### Core Technologies

- **PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers)**
- **SCADA systems**
- **Sensors & actuators**
- **Industrial networks** (Modbus, Profibus, Ethernet/IP)
- **Control algorithms**

|Aspect|Robotics|Automation|
|---|---|---|
|Focus|Physical robots|Processes & control|
|Mobility|Often mobile or articulated|Usually fixed systems|
|Intelligence|Higher (AI, perception)|Rule-based logic|
|Example|Robot arm, drone|Automated production line|
# II. Ethical aspect
## Ethical Aspects of Robotics and Automation

The rapid development of robotics and automation raises **major ethical questions** about how these technologies affect **humans, society, and decision-making**. Ethics in this field focuses on ensuring that technology is **safe, fair, transparent, and beneficial** to humanity.

1. Job Displacement and Social Inequality
2. Responsibility and Accountability
3. Safety and Reliability
4. Privacy and Surveillance
5. Bias and Discrimination
6. Autonomous Weapons and Military Use
7. 9. Environmental Ethics


# III. Benefits and stackholders
## 1. Benefits of Robotics and Automation

### Economic & Industrial Benefits
- Increased productivity and efficiency
- Reduced operational costs in the long term
- High precision and consistent quality
- 24/7 operation without fatigue

### Social & Human Benefits

- Improved workplace safety
- Reduction of dangerous and repetitive tasks
- Support for healthcare (surgery, prosthetics, assistance)
- Better quality of life for elderly and disabled people

### Technological & Environmental Benefits

- Faster innovation and technological advancement
- Optimized use of energy and resources
- Reduced waste through intelligent automation


## 2. Stakeholders in Robotics and Automation

Stakeholders are **all actors affected by or involved in these technologies**.

### A. Primary Stakeholders

1. **Engineers & Developers**
    
    - Design and program robots and automation systems
    - Responsible for safety, reliability, and ethical design
2. **Manufacturers & Companies**
    
    - Deploy systems to increase efficiency and profit
    - Must ensure compliance with laws and safety standards

3. **Users / Operators**
    
    - Interact directly with robots
    - Need training and clear interfaces
        
4. **Workers & Employees**
    
    - Affected by automation in the workplace
    - May require reskilling and job adaptation
        

---

### B. Secondary Stakeholders

5. **Governments & Regulators**
    
    - Create laws, standards, and policies
    - Protect public safety and rights
        
6. **Society & Citizens**
    
    - Impacted by economic, social, and ethical consequences
    - Concerned with privacy, safety, and fairness
        
7. **Researchers & Academics**
    
    - Advance technology responsibly
    - Study risks and societal impact


# IV. Regulation in the domain
## 4. Regulations and Legal Frameworks

Regulations ensure **safe, lawful, and ethical deployment**.

### Safety Regulations

- Mandatory testing and certification
- Emergency stop and fail-safe mechanisms
- Risk assessment before deployment
    

### Data Protection & Privacy

- Limits on data collection
- User consent
- Secure storage and transmission of data
    

### Accountability & Liability

- Clear responsibility in case of failure or harm
- Legal liability for manufacturers and operators
    

### International Standards (Examples)

- **ISO** – robot safety standards
- **IEC** – electrical and system safety
- **EU AI regulations** – trustworthy and explainable AI
- **Labor laws** – worker protection
